# Changes

## Unreleased

This version keeps every function and argument of earlier versions. Alignments run much faster, several bugs are fixed, and `LDDMM` and `LDDMM_3D_to_slice` choose more of their settings from the data (Round 2 below), so results differ from earlier ones. `legacy_defaults=True` turns the Round 2 defaults off.

### Round 2: better default alignments

`LDDMM` and `LDDMM_3D_to_slice` now start with a coarse affine pre-alignment, and choose the regularization weight `sigmaR` and (in 2D) the step sizes from the images. On the held-out problems of the evaluation suite (synthetic sections with a known deformation, never used for tuning, all run on the same machine type and paired with the previous version), the geometric mean error of the alignment fell from 27.5 to 11.9 µm in 2D (ratio 0.43, ±2 SE 0.27–0.70; better on 21 of 27 problems, worse on 2; median ratio 0.87) and from 124 to 26 µm in 3D (ratio 0.21, 0.08–0.51; better on 4 of 4). On 15 further held-out problems with other combinations of deformation size, realism and pixel size, the ratios were 0.37 in 2D (0.18–0.78; 10 better, 2 worse of 13) and 0.28 in 3D (2 problems; not significant). The large gains come from alignments that failed before: rotated sections, slices tilted by a few degrees, and the synthetic Xenium sections, on which the old step sizes were unstable. Round 2 also matches landmarks under the fitted map, and adds `align_cells` for two sets of cells, a `blur_um` option for the rasterization kernel and an optional mixture model for targets with missing tissue.

- **Affine pre-fit** (`prefit='auto'`). LDDMM's gradient descent does not correct a large initial misalignment: it failed on test sections rotated by 20–30° and never corrected atlas planes tilted by a few degrees. Now, before LDDMM, many starts are fitted as affine maps on coarse (about 80 pixels across) images, and the best one is refined and handed to LDDMM. In 2D the starts are the initial affine rotated in 12 steps of 30° about the target's tissue centroid; in 3D the initial plane tilted by -6, 0 and 6° about both in-plane axes and shifted by -300, 0 and 300 µm along its normal. Ties (energies within 1%) go to the start closest to the initial affine, so nearly symmetric tissue keeps its orientation. In 2D the pre-fit is skipped when landmarks are given, and in both from a nonzero initial velocity `v` (a warm start); `prefit=True` or `False` forces it. It takes 1–3 s per 2D tutorial and about 4.5 s per 3D tutorial (Apple M1 Ultra, 4 threads), is deterministic, and is reported in the result's `'prefit'` entry.
- **sigmaR from a displacement scale** (`sigmaR=None`, new `sigma_disp=94.03`). A fixed `sigmaR` means a different deformation prior at every pixel size, intensity scale and smoothness `a`: on the evaluation suite the best `sigmaR` moved with the pixel size. The default `sigmaR` is now `sigma_disp · sqrt(C π) · a^(d/2) · sqrt(nt) · (dx / 30 µm) · sigmaM / s_J` (C = 12 in 2D, 64 in 3D; dx the target pixel size in source units; s_J the typical tissue intensity of the target), so the prior allows displacements of about `sigma_disp` µm whatever the images. At the defaults, a 30 µm target with `s_J = sigmaM` gets about the previous 2D default 5e5. It assumes coordinates in µm. A given `sigmaR` is used as is. This replaces the 3D default `sigmaR=2e6` below.
- **2D step sizes from the data** (`epL`, `epT`, `epV` = None). The previous defaults are scaled by `sigmaM² · 1.1 / G`, where G estimates the curvature of the image term from the target's variance and the source's gradients after the pre-fit. Fixed steps were unstable on some images and needlessly small on others. Given values are used as is. 3D steps are unchanged.
- **2000 iterations by default in 2D** (`niter=None`, previously 5000). With the new steps, stopping at 2000 instead of 5000 iterations changed the error by 0.4% (±2 SE −0.2 to +1.0%) in 2.5 times less time.
- **Landmarks are matched under the fitted map** (`LDDMM` with `pointsI` and `pointsJ`). The landmark term moved `pointsI` by forward Euler steps, which invert the fitted target-to-source map only approximately, so the fit matched landmark positions that differ from those `transform_points_source_to_target` gives after the fit. Each time step is now solved exactly (fixed-point iteration and a Newton step; the gradient follows from the implicit function theorem), in both engines. With the other Round 2 defaults, on the MERFISH-to-Visium tutorial problem the error of its 12 landmarks fell from 75.4 to 70.1 µm, and when 4 of them were held out of the fit, their error fell from 272.8 to 268.1 µm. The Xenium-to-Xenium tutorial is unchanged (28.5 µm), and on Xenium-to-H&E the change (114.3 to 111.7 µm) is within that problem's sensitivity to rounding. A `RuntimeWarning` reports steps that did not converge.
- **Caveat: targets with missing tissue.** Where the target lacks tissue that the source has (partial sections), the new defaults did not help: of 8 such test problems, 3 improved (by 14–78%) and 5 got worse (by 9–62%). Check such alignments, and compare with `legacy_defaults=True` or `prefit=False`, or try `mixture='em'` (below).
- `legacy_defaults=True` (keyword-only) restores the defaults before Round 2: no pre-fit (unless `prefit=True`), `niter=5000`, `epL=2e-8`, `epT=2e-1`, `epV=2e3`, `sigmaR=5e5` in 2D and `sigmaR=2e6` in 3D, and forward Euler steps in the landmark term. The results are then bit for bit those without the Round 2 changes (the corrections below still apply).
- The result has two new entries: `'prefit'` (whether the pre-fit ran, the affine `'A'` the fit started from, which is the initial affine when the pre-fit did not run, and if it ran the energy of every start) and `'settings'` (the `niter`, `sigmaR`, `epL`, `epT` and `epV` the fit used). Passing them back, with `A=out['prefit']['A']` in place of `L`, `T` or `A` and `prefit=False` (and `legacy_defaults=True` if the fit used it), repeats the fit exactly, for example to align several sections with the same settings.
- **New function `align_cells(xI, yI, xJ, yJ)`** aligns two sets of cells in one call. It rasterizes both at 15 µm with a Gaussian kernel of 30 µm standard deviation and runs `LDDMM` coarse to fine on mean-pooled copies of these images (500 iterations at 60 µm pixels, then 500 at 30 µm), each level starting from the previous level's affine and velocity field, with `LDDMM`'s defaults at each level. It returns `LDDMM`'s result, so the transform functions work on it as usual, plus the two rasters. On the 27 held-out synthetic cell-to-cell problems of our evaluation suite (known deformations of real MERFISH, Xenium and heart cell sets), this workflow had less than half the error of `LDDMM` with the new defaults on the suite's own rasters (15, 30 or 60 µm pixels with a kernel tied to the pixel size): geometric mean 5.2 instead of 11.9 µm (better on 24 problems), in half the fitting time. It was slightly less accurate only where the cells were already rasterized at 15 µm (3.4 instead of 3.0 µm, for example). Partial tissue remains hard: on a sparse heart section missing 20% of its cells the error stays above that of the initial affine. Images from other sources (H&E, Visium, an atlas) still go to `LDDMM` directly.
- `rasterize` and `rasterize_with_signal` take a keyword-only `blur_um`: the kernel standard deviation in the units of the cell coordinates, instead of `blur` in pixels (`blur = blur_um/(2*dx)`), so the kernel keeps its physical width at any pixel size. `blur` now defaults to `None`, which means 1.0 unless `blur_um` is given; giving both is an error. Calls without `blur_um` give the same images as before.
- **New option `LDDMM(mixture='em')`** (keyword-only, 2D, off by default): a mixture model that estimates the noise of the matching term from the residual and gives a low matching weight to target pixels that look like background where the deformed source has tissue, as where the target section is incomplete. From iteration 50, every 5 iterations, each target pixel is weighted between the matching class and a background class whose mean is the median of the target's border (or `muB`); a dict changes these choices (see the docstring). In the evaluation (on top of the Round 2 defaults and a smoother regularization kernel that was not adopted), it cut the error on the 8 partial-section test problems to a quarter (all 8 better) and left the others unchanged on average, but made the 4 problems with uneven cell density 9% worse, so it is not the default: try it where the target lacks tissue. Both engines implement it natively, at no measurable cost per iteration. `LDDMM_3D_to_slice` does not have it.

### Faster

- `LDDMM` and `LDDMM_3D_to_slice` have a new optional C++ engine, compiled by `pip` when a C++17 compiler is available. Without one, they use a new pure `torch` implementation. Both compute the same iterations up to rounding. Speed per iteration on an Apple M1 Ultra (16 threads) against the previous version, with the resulting time for the tutorial's fit:

  | | C++ engine | `torch` engine |
  |---|---|---|
  | 2D, MERFISH tutorial (10,000 iterations: ~14 min before) | 81–91x (~11 s) | ~10x (~1.6 min) |
  | 3D, MERFISH-to-Allen tutorial (2,000 iterations: ~22 min before) | 39–45x (~35 s) | ~10x (~2.5 min) |

- `LDDMM` and `LDDMM_3D_to_slice` draw their figures once, after the last iteration, instead of every 10 iterations (`draw=True` restores the old behavior).
- `rasterize` is about 50x faster (86,000 cells: 2 s to 35 ms; 2 million cells: 47 s to 0.85 s), and `rasterize_with_signal` about 60x with one signal column and about 20x with 16 columns. Both give the same images up to rounding (1e-12 relative). Peak memory is bounded.
- The transform functions split large point sets into chunks and use several threads.

### New options

- `LDDMM` and `LDDMM_3D_to_slice` take keyword-only arguments `backend` (`'auto'`, `'cpp'` or `'torch'`), `num_threads` and `draw`. The new `backend_info()` reports which engine is used and, if the C++ engine is unavailable, why.
- `LDDMM` and `LDDMM_3D_to_slice` take a keyword-only argument `affine_step_control` (default `False` in `LDDMM` and `True` in `LDDMM_3D_to_slice`, see below).
- The number of threads is at most `num_threads`, else the environment variable `STALIGN_NUM_THREADS`, else `torch.get_num_threads()`. Results do not depend on it.
- `transform_points_source_to_target`, `build_transform(direction='f')`, `build_transform3D(direction='f')` and `transform_image_target_to_source` take `exact_inverse` (default `True`, see below).

### Changes that affect results

- **Source-to-target transforms use the exact inverse of the fitted mapping.** `LDDMM` fits a target-to-source map; mapping cells the other way previously used a forward Euler approximation that is not its inverse. On the tutorial fits, cells move by 1–13 µm on average and up to ~100 µm. The ground-truth error of the simulation tutorial drops from 9.68 to 6.68 µm. `exact_inverse=False` gives the previous approximation.
- **`transform_points_target_to_source` integrates the velocity in the correct time order** (t = nt-1 down to 0, as `LDDMM` and `build_transform` do). Changes are small: at most 0.26 raster pixels on the tutorials.
- **The 3D regularizer is corrected.** `LDDMM_3D_to_slice` transformed the velocity over only two of its three axes, so it applied no smoothness left-to-right and a position-dependent penalty up to 83,521 times stronger at the midline. It now computes the documented 0.5/sigmaR² Σ_t ‖L v_t‖². At the old default `sigmaR=1e8` the corrected term regularizes much less than the old one did (in the 3D tutorials the rms velocity became up to twice as large), so the default became `sigmaR=2e6`, which gives deformations of about the previous size (rms velocity 16.7 and 17.4 µm in the MERFISH and STARmap tutorials, against 15.7 and 14.7 µm before). With Round 2 the default is chosen from the data (`legacy_defaults=True` gives 2e6). `sigmaR=1e8` gives the old value.
- **`LDDMM_3D_to_slice` controls the step of each affine parameter** (`affine_step_control=True`, the new default). With the default `epT`, the translation found by plain gradient descent never settled: it jumped back and forth by 45–165 µm per iteration until the last one, so the result depended on rounding. Now the step of each entry of `L` and `T` is multiplied by its own factor between 0.001 and 1, which halves when that parameter's gradient changes sign from one iteration to the next and grows by 5% per iteration while it does not, so steps are never larger than `epL` and `epT`. The affine parameters converge (the translation moves by 0.001–0.3 µm per iteration at the end), and changing the initial translation in its last digit moves the cells by about 1e-12 µm instead of 30–84 µm. With the new `sigmaR`, the 3D tutorial alignments match the atlas about as well as the previous version did:

  | | matching energy | cells in brain | Dice (tissue vs brain) | correlation with the atlas |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | MERFISH, previous version | 29,095 | 97.1% | 0.885 | 0.394 |
  | MERFISH, this version | 30,169 | 96.9% | 0.882 | 0.400 |
  | STARmap, previous version | 92,564 | 97.5% | 0.804 | 0.516 |
  | STARmap, this version | 77,782 | 97.7% | 0.805 | 0.522 |

  (Matching energy: EM over the last 20 iterations, lower is better. Correlation: the target with the deformed atlas Nissl image over the tissue.) `affine_step_control=False` gives the plain gradient steps. The control is off by default in `LDDMM`: on the 2D tutorials the plain steps either already converge (MERFISH: the cells move by 0.03 µm with the control) or the control made the landmark error worse (the noisy H&E target of the Xenium tutorial: 34.2 µm instead of 30.3 µm).
- **`analyze3Dalign` returns correct atlas coordinates.** It indexed a 10-unit grid with indices computed from `dx`, so coordinates were wrong whenever `dx` was not 10 (by 4.3 mm on average in the STARmap tutorial, `dx=50`) and biased by up to 19 µm otherwise. Region labels were mostly right and change for under 3% of cells at region boundaries.
- `LDDMM` and `LDDMM_3D_to_slice` return the affine `A`, velocity `v` and (3D) sample points `Xs` of the same, last evaluated iteration. `v` previously came from one update later than `A`. The difference is one velocity step.
- `muA` and `muB` are matched to the number of target channels. A 3-entry mean against a 1-channel target (as in the 3D tutorials) previously counted the squared distance three times; it now warns and uses the single value. Integer, float, list, numpy and torch values are accepted.
- `rasterize_with_signal` keeps the sign of a signed signal (it returned the absolute value). `normalize` maps a constant array to `t_min` instead of NaN, so `rasterize` with a constant gene gives a zero image instead of NaN.

Long 3D fits with plain affine steps are very sensitive to rounding: a change in the last digit of the input moved the previous version's result by 30–84 µm on average in the 3D tutorials. Small differences from earlier versions are therefore expected even where nothing was fixed. With the affine step control, the new default in 3D, the same change moves the result by about 1e-12 µm, and the C++ and `torch` engines give the same 3D tutorial alignments to within 1e-11 µm. 2D alignments of noisy images can still be sensitive: the same change moves the cells of the Xenium-to-H&E tutorial by 8 µm on average.

### Bug fixes

- `rasterizePCA` works (it raised `NameError: name 'tools' is not defined`) and tolerates genes without counts.
- `build_transform3D(direction='f')` works (it always raised an error) and accepts `v=None`.
- `v_to_phii` and `v_to_phii_3D` accept the velocity layout that `LDDMM` returns.
- `calculate_tre` and the transform functions accept numpy arrays, torch tensors and lists of integers or floats.
- `point_annotator.py` and `curve_annotator.py` work with output file names (they raised `NameError`).
- `saveRasters` writes into the folder `path` (it wrote next to it without a trailing slash).
- `download_aba_ontology` and `download_aba_image_labels` raise on HTTP errors instead of saving the error page.
- `plot_brain_regions` gives every region its own color with a readable legend; both plot helpers return `(fig, ax)`.
- `LDDMM_3D_to_slice` warns that it ignores `pointsI` and `pointsJ` (it has no landmark term).
- Inputs are checked before fitting, with error messages that name the problem. A drawing error warns instead of stopping the fit.

### Packaging

- `requirements.txt` lists only the runtime dependencies, unpinned: matplotlib, numpy, pandas, pynrrd, requests and torch. pandas, pynrrd and requests are needed only by the Allen atlas helpers. The tutorials also use plotly, scipy, scikit-learn and scikit-image.
- `pyproject.toml` declares the build requirements. pybind11 is needed only to compile the C++ engine; if compilation fails, installation still succeeds and the `torch` engine is used.
- A `pytest` suite in `tests/` checks both engines against a plain-autograd reference implementation of the iteration (`tests/reference.py`), and the rasterization and transforms against the previous version.
